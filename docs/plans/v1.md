# Plan: Metro tax burden, v1

Status: accepted, with decisions recorded in section 13. Last updated: 2026-11-20. Build plan: [v1-build.md](v1-build.md).

## 1. Purpose

Estimate the total state and local tax burden a household would pay in each U.S. metro area, given a wage (or the metro's median wage when none is entered), filing status, housing choice, and user-entered taxable spending. Show results as dollars, effective rate, and hours of work.

## 2. Scope for v1

Included:

- Federal income tax and FICA. With a user-entered wage this is the same in every metro; with the median-wage fallback it varies by metro (section 13.2)
- State income tax, by state
- Local income tax, for the jurisdictions that levy it, listed explicitly
- Property tax for owners, observed at metro level from ACS, with a scenario for home value. Renters show $0 with a pass-through note (section 13.5)
- Sales tax, combined state and local, weighted across the metro's counties, applied to user-entered taxable spending only (section 13.6)
- Standard deduction and personal exemption for federal and state income tax (section 13.1)

Excluded in v1:

- Itemized deductions, including the federal SALT deduction, and tax credits. Stated as a limitation, with an option to add later.
- Filing statuses other than Single and Married filing jointly (section 13.3)
- Non-wage income
- Payroll add-ons such as state paid leave programs, unless they're small enough to include without new data work. Listed as a later phase.
- Address-level tax rates
- Tax advice of any kind

## 3. Burden definitions

Show both, with labels:

- **Effective rate:** Total tax ÷ gross income
- **Hours of work:** Total tax ÷ hourly wage, expressed per month

The wage is the user's entry (hourly, or annual ÷ 2,080) or, if none is entered, the metro's median hourly wage, labeled as such (section 13.2).

## 4. Metro representation

- Metros are defined by OMB as sets of whole counties, so county-level data aggregates cleanly to a metro.
- State taxes are exact for each state.
- Local income tax is applied by jurisdiction. Each metro has a flag showing whether a local income tax applies, and the list of jurisdictions included.
- Sales tax is a weighted average across the metro's counties or cities, weighted by population. The result is an approximation and is labeled that way.
- Property tax comes from ACS median real estate taxes paid divided by median home value, observed at metro level.

## 5. Local income tax list (v1)

Include only jurisdictions with a confirmed current ordinance. Starting candidates, to verify before use:

- New York City
- Philadelphia, and Pennsylvania municipalities with earned income taxes
- Cities in Ohio with municipal income taxes
- Detroit and other Michigan cities with income taxes
- Maryland counties with local income taxes
- Kentucky cities with occupational license taxes
- Missouri cities with earnings taxes (for example Kansas City and St. Louis)
- Oregon cities with income taxes (for example Portland, and others)

Each entry records: jurisdiction, rate, tax year, source URL, and as-of date. Metros without any included jurisdiction show "none listed," not "none."

## 6. Data tiers

- **Observed:** ACS property tax paid, median home value, median rent, median wage
- **Estimated:** Weighted sales tax rate, metro-level approximation
- **Scenario:** The overall burden calculation, which depends on user inputs and stated assumptions

The tier system from the groceries and mortgage plan applies unchanged.

## 7. Tax-year handling

- Store the tax year with every table.
- Show the tax year on every result.
- A test suite fails when a bracket table changes, so an annual update is a deliberate step.

## 8. Accuracy testing

- Test every state's income tax against the state revenue department's worksheet or published example for at least one filing status.
- Test federal and FICA calculations against IRS published figures.
- For the local income tax list, test each jurisdiction against its ordinance text.
- Keep a visible note: "Estimate for planning. Not tax advice."

## 9. Licensing

- Federal, state, and local government sources: public domain or the jurisdiction's open data terms. Record each one in DATA.md.
- Third-party compilations, such as combined sales tax rate datasets: check terms before use. Prefer primary sources.
- Code: MIT. Data: under each source's license, as in the time-to-afford repo.

## 10. Privacy

- Wage, housing choice, and taxable spending are computed in the browser.
- Nothing user-entered goes into URLs or leaves the browser.

## 11. Build order

1. Data schema with tier, source, license, and tax-year fields.
2. Federal and FICA (fixed values) and state income tax for all states, with tests.
3. ACS property tax by metro, for about ten metros, verified against Census tables.
4. Sales tax weighting for the same ten metros.
5. Local income tax list, starting with the jurisdictions above, verified one by one.
6. Burden calculation, with both definitions.
7. Front end: inputs, metro selection, results with labels and tier badges.

## 13. Decisions (2026-11-20)

1. **Deductions.** Federal and state income tax apply the standard deduction and any personal exemption for the selected filing status. Itemized deductions, the SALT deduction and credits are excluded and listed as limitations. This makes results comparable with the state worksheets used in section 8.
2. **Wage fallback.** When no wage is entered, each metro uses its own median hourly wage (BLS OEWS, all occupations), and every result says so, for example "Using the median wage for this metro: $X/hour". Because of this, federal tax and FICA vary by metro under the fallback and are identical across metros only when the user enters a wage. An entered wage replaces the median everywhere.
3. **Filing status.** A selection, defaulting to **Single**. v1 offers Single and Married filing jointly; each bracket table stores values for both. Other statuses are a later phase.
4. **Tax year.** Tax year 2026 brackets, deductions and rates, used with ACS 2025 1-year data. Results show both periods and label the mismatch.
5. **Renters and property tax.** Renters show **$0** property tax, labeled clearly, with the note: "Renters don't pay property tax directly, but some of the landlord's property tax may be passed through in rent. That share isn't estimated."
6. **Taxable spending.** No default. The user must enter an annual taxable spending amount. Until they do, sales tax and any total that includes it show as "Enter taxable spending to calculate" rather than a number, and the components that don't depend on it are still shown.
